ijji · Your business buddy around the corner

Your business buddy, tuned into your shop and the area around it.

ijji helps restaurant and café owners get to the likely cause of a shop problem. It works with whatever signals are available—from the shop itself, nearby people, competitors, reviews, and the menu—then turns them into practical daily actions you can measure and refine over time, all through LINE.

psychologyWhy shop owners need ijji

You can see the symptoms. Finding the cause is harder.

Small shops often have to act on guesswork—run a promotion, cut prices, change the menu—before anyone knows where the leak is. ijji starts by separating symptoms from possible causes, then helps you choose one thing worth doing first.

mapYour neighbourhood · What ijji reads before answering

Foot traffic is not the same as sales.

ijji looks beyond the name of the neighbourhood or the number of people passing by. It asks who those people are, when they arrive, whether they can stop to buy, and whether the shop offers something simple enough to choose in that moment. At minimum, the people around a shop fall into three groups, each creating opportunity at a different time of day.

homePeople who live nearby
They return as part of a routine, with different patterns on weekdays and weekends—useful for repeat visits, regular favourites, and short delivery radiuses close to home.
luggagePeople visiting the area
They come for an occasion, often in groups, and search before arriving—useful for signature dishes, strong photos and reviews, weekends, and little indulgences.
commutePeople passing through
They move through at particular times and in a particular direction, often in a hurry—useful for quick ordering, grab-and-go, breakfast, lunch, and after work.

Four more questions ijji asks before calling it an opportunity

  • Can they stop to buy?Are they ready to buy, or simply passing by?
  • What can they realistically buy then?Breakfast, a quick break, or something after work?
  • Can they see and enter the shop easily?Consider the sign, walking route, parking, and opening hours.
  • Is there an easier choice nearby?A competitor that is clearer and easier to choose.

Here is how ijji would put it

“Lots of people nearby is encouraging, but it does not automatically mean sales. We still need to ask whether they can stop and buy—and whether your shop has an option simple enough to fit the moment they are in.”

chatA sample answer from ijji

Grounded in your shop and neighbourhood—not generic advice.

Every answer begins with what the owner shares. ijji looks for the likely cause before offering advice, states how confident it is, and ends with something you can do today plus the number to watch.

This example is based on a test shop in Khlong San, Bangkok. It demonstrates the format of an answer, not a guaranteed result for your shop.

chat_bubbleWhat you told ijji

Our shop is on a main road. People pass all day, especially in the morning and evening, but very few come in. We have changed the frontage and signs several times without seeing a difference.

lightbulbijji’s leading hypothesis

Morning and evening passers-by may not be sure what the shop sells, what it costs, or whether it is open, so they hesitate to walk in.

We cannot yet conclude that competitors are taking customers. The first action is designed to test the cause before changing the offer—not to discount.

groupsPeople around the shop
Office workers commuting through Sathorn–Silom in the morning and evening · residents of Charoen Nakhon–Khlong San condominiums · visitors heading to ICONSIAM
scheduleKey times
07:00–09:00 · 11:30–13:30 · 17:30–20:00
placeLocation opportunity
After-work commuters and condominium residents looking for a place to pause, with a clearly priced one-dish meal and drink visible immediately from the street.
storeCompetitor gap
Nearby shops focus on quick meals with no place to sit. This shop offers both food and drinks, but still lacks clear prices and an easy-to-understand set visible from the street.
compare_arrowsCompetitors included
Five nearby shops · the owner can edit the list
Action 1 / 7

checklistA pavement sign with three stand-out items and clear prices

There is no need to change the whole shop today. Put a freestanding sign where passers-by can see it at eye level. Show your top-selling dish alongside a signature drink, both with clear prices. From 3–5 metres away, people should understand within three seconds that they can get a filling, affordable meal here.

monitoringMeasure

The number of passers-by who stop to look at the sign for more than three seconds between 17:30 and 19:30.

warningWatch out

Do not cram in so many item names that the sign becomes hard to read. Focus on two or three key items with photos and clear prices.

When it is done, send back one number. ijji will use it to sharpen the next day’s action.

checklist12 shop challenges

Whatever the problem, tell ijji in the way you talk about your shop.

ijji helps separate what should be tested first, what can be acted on now, and what should wait until there is enough evidence.

  1. groups01 · Getting noticed

    Which people nearby should we try to bring in first?

    “People pass all day, but no one turns in.”“What if the people around here were never our customers?”“Who should we focus on first—office workers or people who live in the side streets?”

  2. door_open02 · Walking in

    They can see the shop. Why are they still not buying?

    “People stop to read the menu, then keep walking.”“Are they unsure about the price—or worried the wait will be too long?”“We have a sign and photos. What is still missing?”

  3. add_shopping_cart03 · A little more per order

    What else would this customer group be happy to add?

    “They order one dish, drink water, and leave.”“How do we suggest an add-on without being pushy?”“Would a meal-and-drink set work for them—or only for us?”

  4. schedule04 · Quiet hours

    How can the empty hours earn more?

    “From two to five, every table is empty.”“The lights and air-conditioning are on, the team is ready, but no customers come in.”“Should we close for the afternoon or find something that sells then?”

  5. event_repeat05 · Coming back

    How do we give nearby customers a reason to return?

    “New customers come once, then disappear.”“Which places do people around here return to?”“We tried a loyalty card. They still do not come back.”

  6. sell06 · Price and value

    Which items could carry a price increase and still feel worth it?

    “We are afraid customers will leave if prices rise, but we cannot keep them here.”“Do customers think it is expensive, or do they just not understand what they are getting?”“The shop next door charges more and still fills up.”

  7. restaurant07 · Ingredient costs

    Which dishes should we push—and which need caution?

    “Ingredients cost more every month, but our prices have not moved.”“Is our bestseller also our lowest-margin dish?”“Which items could we remove without customers missing them?”

  8. receipt_long08 · Fixed costs

    Which dayparts could make better use of the rent and opening hours we already pay for?

    “Rent arrives before the sales do, every month.”“Would longer hours cover the extra power and staffing?”“Our costs are the same, but sales are not.”

  9. reviews09 · Reviews and ratings

    What do reviews say we should build on or fix first?

    “One three-star review is still sitting at the top.”“Customers say the food is good, but no one writes a review.”“Should we reply—or leave it alone?”

  10. store10 · Nearby competition

    What could make our shop meaningfully different in customers’ eyes?

    “A new place opened next door and the queue is long.”“What are they doing better—or are they simply easier to notice?”“Do we need to do something they do not, or do the same thing better?”

  11. room_service11 · Service

    Which moments in the service journey stop a purchase or keep people from returning?

    “Food is slow during the rush and customers look frustrated.”“Do we need more people, or just a better queue?”“Which customers genuinely cannot wait?”

  12. trending_up12 · Growing the business

    Where should the shop grow first—and where is the risk?

    “People ask about delivery, but I do not know if it would pay off.”“Do we open a second branch, or fill this one first?”“How can we grow without putting a large sum at risk?”

ijji never claims more certainty than the evidence allows.

Every diagnosis includes a confidence level. When the evidence is not strong enough, the first action is to gather a signal—not to start changing the business.

  • check_circleClear signal

  • hourglass_topEarly signal

    Some evidence supports it—worth a measurable three-day test.

  • helpNot enough information

    Gather more before deciding—the first action is to observe and count.

favoriteFor the people who run the shop

Small shops should not have to figure everything out alone.

Owners are often first to arrive, last to leave, and responsible for nearly every decision—menu, prices, front-of-house, rent, reviews. Too often, those decisions must be made before anyone knows what is really happening. ijji knows how tiring that is. It is not here to lecture, but to offer another view, help set priorities, and stay with the result.

On a good sales day, ijji helps identify what worked. When sales fall, it helps separate what needs attention first. And when the picture is still unclear, ijji says so plainly. Whatever the day brings, ijji is here.
